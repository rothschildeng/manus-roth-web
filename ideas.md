# ROTH DIGITAL — Selected Direction

## Obsidian Gallery

ROTH DIGITAL is presented as a contemporary luxury editorial storefront for digital access. The experience keeps the original product intent—gift cards, game top-ups, subscriptions, creator tools, wallet-first checkout—but shifts the visual language from noisy cyber effects to a curated dark gallery.

The system uses **graphite surfaces**, warm bone typography, brushed-steel cues, and **Aurelia Lime `#C7F34A`** as the only high-energy signal color. The signature motifs are a split-R access mark, thin technical rules, clipped gallery plates, and small orbit labels that make speed and trust feel spatial.

Layout follows an editorial rail rather than a centered marketing grid: asymmetric hero, proof strip, category runway, merchandised catalog, operational method section, wallet layer, and concise closing CTA. Motion is slow and physical—floating hero object, controlled shine, short hover lift—and must respect reduced-motion preferences.

Copy is concise, operational, and high-signal. Preferred phrases include “Access, without the wait,” “Low noise. High signal,” and “One clean handoff.” Avoid generic filler and avoid default purple gradients, excessive rounded cards, and decorative effects that compete with product clarity.

The brand mark is a split geometric R formed from graphite planes and an Aurelia Lime cut, implying a private key and an open doorway. It should repeat at meaningful moments beyond the header so the brand remains recognizable without relying on the wordmark.

## Commerce Experience Map

The public storefront will use a clear route hierarchy: the editorial home page; repository-backed category views; exact product detail views; a persistent cart; payment checkout and payment-status confirmation; customer account and order history; and factual trust, support, and policy pages. Product and category routes must use the imported catalog as the sole source of product labels, displayed prices, variants, and category metadata.

Delivery remains deliberately controlled. A customer can create a payment order and see live confirmation progress, but no delivery action occurs until the administrator approves the confirmed order in the protected review desk. Email or WhatsApp delivery automation will remain unconfigured until a real provider account, approved destination policy, and provider credentials are supplied through secure project configuration.

Trust content must explain the actual manual-review process, payment confirmation status, support route, and policy boundaries without invented claims. Reviews and ratings are absent by default; a verified external review source can be added later, but no testimonial, rating, or customer quote is to be fabricated.
